## The Core Approach: The Census Walk

The biggest mistake in B2B sales is arriving as a salesperson. Crèche owners are busy. They deal with parents, toddlers, inspectors, and grant paperwork every day. They don't have time for a pitch — and they'll tell you so before you finish your sentence.

The Census Walk solves this.

You arrive not as a salesperson but as someone doing something useful: verifying their details for a public directory. This is true — CentreConnect does list crèches publicly. The difference is in how you lead with it.

**How it works:**

1. **Walk in with purpose.** You are confirming their crèche details for the CentreConnect public directory. You are not selling anything yet.
2. **Low-resistance entry.** "Directory verification" feels official, useful, and non-threatening. It opens the door where a cold pitch closes it.
3. **Show them the curiosity hook.** Pull up their listing on centreconnect.co.za. "Is this information correct?" Most people want to see themselves on a website — it creates instant engagement.
4. **Escalate naturally.** Once they're looking at the screen, show the admissions pipeline feature. Then the DOE monthly export. Each step reveals more value without pressure.
5. **Close simply.** "Can we set you up now? Takes 10 minutes."

The whole approach relies on genuine usefulness. You are not pretending to be someone you're not — you are leading with the part of your product that requires nothing from them, then showing them what more is possible.

## The 5-Stage Pipeline

| Stage | Name | What it means | Target time to close |
|-------|------|---------------|----------------------|
| 1 | Identified | You know the crèche exists (DOE list, walk-by, referral, Google Maps) | — |
| 2 | Contacted | First contact made — walk-in, WhatsApp, or referral intro | Day 1 |
| 3 | Curious | They have seen the demo, the listing, or the DOE export | Day 1–2 |
| 4 | Interested | They said "yes, set me up" | Day 1–3 |
| 5 | Active | Account created, onboarding complete, using the app | Within 7 days |

**Stage 1 — Identified**

Build your list before you walk. Use the DOE provincial ECD register, Google Maps searches ("crèche near [township/suburb]"), and referrals from active users. The goal is to know the name and address before you arrive. A warm walk-in beats a cold one.

**Stage 2 — Contacted**

First human contact. This is the hardest part — getting through the door and into a real conversation. Scripts below. The goal of this stage is only to get 90 seconds of attention. Not a sign-up. Not a demo. Just 90 seconds.

**Stage 3 — Curious**

They've seen something on your phone or laptop that made them lean in. The listing, the demo, the export. Curiosity is fragile — don't kill it with a pricing conversation. Keep showing, keep asking questions. Let them sell themselves.

**Stage 4 — Interested**

They said yes. "Set me up." This is a verbal commitment. Don't leave without creating the account or booking a specific follow-up time (same day or next morning). Every hour of delay increases drop-off.

**Stage 5 — Active**

They've logged in, added children, and used at least one core feature. A sign-up without usage is not a win. Stage 5 is only confirmed when they are genuinely using the product. See the "What Active Means" section below.

## Word-for-Word Scripts

These are not suggestions. These are tested phrases. Use them until you find something better, then update this playbook.

---

### Stage 2 — The Opener (Walking in Cold)

Approach the owner or principal directly. Smile. Be calm. Don't apologise for arriving.

> "Good morning, I'm [name] from CentreConnect. We're building a public directory of registered crèches so that parents can find ECD centres near them. We already have your centre listed — I just wanted to confirm your details are correct and show you what parents see when they find you."

**What this does:**
- Establishes who you are immediately
- Leads with benefit to them (parents finding them)
- Creates mild urgency (their listing already exists — is it accurate?)
- Asks for nothing

If they say "I'm busy right now":

> "No problem at all — it'll take two minutes, I just want to show you the listing and make sure your phone number is right. I don't want parents calling the wrong number."

That usually gets you in.

---

### The Curiosity Hook (Showing the Listing)

Pull out your phone. Open centreconnect.co.za. Find their centre. Hand them the phone or turn the screen toward them.

> "Here's how your centre appears to parents searching in this area. Is this information accurate? Is the phone number right? The address?"

Wait. Let them read. Let them react.

Most owners will immediately spot something — a wrong number, a missing detail, a name spelled incorrectly. This is good. It gives them a reason to care.

> "I can update that right now. While I'm here, can I show you something else parents see when they find you?"

This is the natural bridge to the next step. You have not mentioned pricing, subscriptions, or software. You are still helping.

---

### The Pivot to the Platform

Once they're engaged with the listing, move to the platform — but frame it as an extension of what they've already seen, not a product pitch.

> "Centres that are on our full platform also receive digital applications directly. Instead of parents filling in paper forms at the gate, they apply online and you see it here — name, age, which grade, contact details. It goes straight into your register."

Pause. Let that sink in.

> "Would that save you time at enrolment?"

Almost every owner will say yes. Now you have a yes on record — even if it's a small one.

> "It also means you have a waiting list. If a space opens up, you can contact the next parent directly from the system."

You are not selling software. You are solving the problem of the pile of paper forms on their desk.

---

### The DOE Export Close

This is the most powerful moment in the demo. Use it deliberately — save it for after the listing and the admissions pitch have landed.

> "One more thing — do you do your DOE monthly return manually? The form you submit to the district each month?"

They will almost certainly say yes. Some will groan.

> "Can I show you something?"

Open the DOE export in the app. Show them a generated monthly report — children's names, ages, attendance, gender breakdown — pre-filled, ready to print or email.

> "This takes about three minutes on our system instead of hours. All the information you've already captured — attendance, children's details — automatically fills the form. You just download it and submit."

Then:

> "Want to see how it works for your centre's data?"

If they say yes, you are at Stage 3. The demo has landed.

---

### The Soft Close

Only use this once they've seen the DOE export or the admissions pipeline — after genuine curiosity has been established. Do not jump here early.

> "We're offering a Founders Pilot right now. Your onboarding and first month are completely free — no card, nothing upfront. After that it's R299 a month, which works out to about R10 a day. Can I set you up now? It takes 10 minutes."

Say it simply and then stop talking. Do not add qualifiers. Do not fill the silence. Let them respond.

If they say yes, open the sign-up flow immediately. Right there. Don't say "great, I'll send you a link." Do it now.

---

### Handling Objections

**"I don't have time right now."**

> "Completely fine. When would be a good time — tomorrow morning before the children arrive, or this afternoon after pickup? I'll come to you."

Book a specific time before you leave. A vague "I'll follow up" leads nowhere. A time in their calendar gives you a second chance.

---

**"We already do it on Excel."**

> "That's exactly who we built this for. The difference is that our system automatically formats the DOE monthly return for you — you don't have to copy anything across. Your Excel becomes this."

Show the DOE export again if needed.

> "The data entry is the same. The output just happens automatically."

---

**"Is it safe? Who sees our children's data?"**

Take this seriously. Don't brush it off.

> "That's an important question and I'm glad you asked. All data is stored securely — it's not shared with anyone, not sold, not visible to other centres. Only you and people you give access to can see your children's records. We comply with POPIA. I can send you our privacy policy if you'd like to read it."

Then:

> "No other parent, no other centre, no advertiser — nobody. Your data is yours."

---

**"R299 is too expensive."**

> "I hear you. The Founders Pilot gives you the first month completely free so you can see for yourself whether it saves you enough time to be worth it. Most principals tell me they get back at least an hour a week on admin. That's time you can spend with the children."

If they're still hesitant:

> "What would it need to do to be worth R299 a month for you?"

This question tells you exactly what they value most — and lets you show them that feature directly.

---

**"Let me think about it."**

This is the most common delay. Don't push — but don't leave empty-handed.

> "Of course. What questions do you still have? I want to make sure you have everything you need to make the decision."

If they have no specific questions:

> "Can I follow up with you on Friday? I'll send you a WhatsApp with our website and a short video so you can show your husband/partner/board."

Then follow up on Friday. On the dot.

## After the Close — Onboarding Checklist

Do this in the first 10 minutes, right there at the crèche. Do not leave it for later.

1. **Create their account.** Email address + centre name. Use their phone if they don't have a laptop handy.
2. **Set onboarding_complete = true** in the CC Admin dashboard in Supabase. This activates their account and starts the Founders Pilot clock.
3. **Add 3 children together.** Don't just show them — make them do it. Type the first child's name yourself as a demo, then hand the phone over for the second and third. Doing it once makes it real.
4. **Mark attendance.** Show them how it works. Have them tap a child present. Takes 20 seconds. Now they've done it.
5. **Run the DOE export.** Let them see their own data in the report. Even with just 3 children it lands better than any demo.
6. **Send them the login link on WhatsApp** before you leave. Type a short message: "Hi [name], here's your CentreConnect login: [link]. Call me if anything — [your number]."

If the onboarding takes longer than 15 minutes, something is wrong. Simplify.

## Referral Activation

Wait until a crèche has been active for at least 2 weeks before asking for referrals. Ask too early and they don't have enough experience to recommend you with confidence.

**The ask:**

> "You've been using the system for a couple of weeks now — how's it going?"

Listen. Then:

> "Do you know any other crèche owners nearby who still do their DOE return manually? I'd love to show them what you're using."

If they hesitate:

> "Even just someone you've chatted to at a district meeting or a workshop — I'll do the legwork, I just need a name."

**The incentive:**

> "If you refer someone and they join, I'll give you one month free. I'll apply it to your account the day they sign up."

Keep the incentive simple and immediate. Don't promise vague future rewards.

**The testimonial ask:**

> "Can I ask a favour? Could you record a 30-second voice note on WhatsApp telling me what you use the system for and whether you'd recommend it? I won't post anything without your permission — it just helps other principals trust us when they're deciding."

A voice note from a real crèche owner in the same neighbourhood is worth more than any written marketing you can produce.

## Weekly GTM Rhythm

| Day | Activity |
|-----|----------|
| Monday | Review the full pipeline. Move every Stage 3 and 4 contact — follow up by phone or WhatsApp. If someone said "let me think about it" last week, today is the day to go back. |
| Tuesday | Census walks. Minimum 3 new crèches. Aim for 5. Track every visit in your pipeline log even if they weren't interested. |
| Wednesday | Census walks continue. Prioritise areas with clusters of crèches — one street can have 3 or 4. |
| Thursday | Onboarding visits for anyone who signed up earlier in the week. In-person where possible. Remote via WhatsApp video if necessary. |
| Friday | WhatsApp check-ins with all active pilots. Ask: "How's it going this week? Anything you need help with?" This is also when you follow up on "let me think about it" promises made on Monday. |

Consistency matters more than intensity. Five crèches a week for eight weeks is forty conversations. That's a pipeline.

| Metric | This Week | Last Week |

| New crèches identified | | |
| First contacts made | | |

| Accounts created (Stage 4+) | | |
| Active users (logged in this week) | | |
| Referrals asked for | | |
| Referrals received | | |

**What to watch:**

- If contacts are high but demos are low — your opener isn't landing. Practise the curiosity hook.
- If demos are high but accounts are low — the close is the problem. Revisit the soft close script and objection handling.
- If accounts are created but active users are low — onboarding is breaking down. Go back in person.

## What "Active" Means

A crèche that has an account but never uses it is not a customer. It is a number on a spreadsheet.

A crèche is only truly active when all three of these have happened:

- [ ] Attendance marked at least once
- [ ] DOE export generated at least once
- [ ] At least one parent has received a notification from the system

If a crèche has not done all three within 14 days of signing up, contact them. WhatsApp first. If no response within 24 hours, call. If no response, go in person.

Most drop-off at this stage comes from one thing: they forgot their login details, or they opened the app once and didn't know what to do next. A five-minute in-person visit fixes this almost every time.

Do not wait for them to come to you.

## Notes on Selling in South African ECD Contexts

A few things that matter here and don't appear in generic sales playbooks:

**Language.** If the owner speaks isiZulu, Sesotho, or Afrikaans as a first language, use it — even a greeting in their language changes the temperature of the conversation. You don't need to be fluent. You need to not be a stranger.

**Trust takes time in townships.** A first visit may produce nothing except a warm reception and a name. That is still progress. Come back. Consistency builds trust faster than any pitch.

**The DOE compliance angle is universal.** Every registered ECD centre has to submit a monthly return. Every single one. This is the most universal pain point regardless of suburb, township, or province. Lead with it when the listing hook doesn't land.

**Principals are often the teacher, receptionist, and admin person.** They are not resistant because they don't see the value — they are resistant because they genuinely have no time. Honour that. Do the setup for them where you can. Make starting require zero effort on their part.

**R299 is not trivial in every context.** In a township crèche running on government subsidies, R299 a month is a real decision. The Founders Pilot free month is essential here — it removes the risk. Reinforce that they pay nothing until they've decided it's worth it.
